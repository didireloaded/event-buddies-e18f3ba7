## The verdict first

**DICE is the single best app to steal from for Event Buddies, and the reason is narrow: DICE solved the exact problem you have not solved yet, which is making a text-heavy event page feel like a night out instead of a brochure.** Its 4.9 App Store rating is not from features, it is from *presentation*. Steal its visual and structural discipline, not its ticketing.

---

## Tier 1: Steal these three things today

### 1. Posters as the interface, not decoration

DICE treats the event flyer as the primary UI element, not a thumbnail. The discovery feed is a vertical stack of near-full-width cards where artist photography bleeds edge to edge, with venue, date, and price set in heavy condensed sans directly over or beneath the image. There is almost no chrome between the user and the lineup. The app trusts the imagery to sell the night, and typographic contrast (huge headliner name, small supporting metadata) does the ranking work.

**Translation for Event Buddies:** your Home screen currently has no event image at all, just text. Steal this immediately. The Sound of Summer card should be a full-bleed poster with the event name in heavy type over the image, not a bullet list of bars. Your voice note already asked for "a small picture of Summer, One Coast stage" — DICE proves that picture should be the *whole* card, not a thumbnail in a text block.

### 2. "Browse First" before any account

DICE's onboarding explicitly includes a 'Browse First' option, which lets users explore the app's content before committing to creating an account. This is not a nice-to-have, it is why their conversion works. The app earns the signup *after* the user finds something they are excited about.

**Translation for Event Buddies:** you already planned guest-first (name only, no email). DICE validates the sequence but pushes it further: **let the user browse the event lineup and map before you ask for their name.** Show the anchor screen ("You're going to Sound of Summer"), let them tap into the lineup, and only ask for a name when they try to start a crew or drop a pin. The name request becomes contextual, not a gate.

### 3. A fixed bottom primary action, always visible

On DICE's event detail page, the bottom navigation bar shows the lowest ticket price alongside a bright "Get Tickets" button, communicating the next step at all times. The primary action never scrolls away.

**Translation for Event Buddies:** every event-scoped screen needs a fixed bottom action. On the event hub: **"Join your crew"** or **"I'm going."** On the map: **"Drop a pin."** On crew home: **"Lost someone 🆘."** Right now your app has no persistent CTA anywhere, which is why it feels passive. The action bar is the difference between a page and a tool.

## Tier 2: Steal these patterns

| DICE pattern | Why it works | Event Buddies translation |
|---|---|---|
| **Map as a discovery path, not just wayfinding** | DICE offers list, filter, and map as parallel ways to find shows. The critique notes their map button is too hidden and should be more prominent. | Your map tab should be a *primary* surface, not a secondary view. Make it first-class: search bar, filter chips (Stages, Bars, Food, Medic, Gate), and tap-to-expand detail sheets. Do not hide it behind a "View map" corner button. |
| **Pill-shaped filter chips** | DICE places pills for date, price, and city under the search bar, mirroring how people naturally plan. | Same pattern for your event map and lineup: `All · Stages · Bars · Toilets · Food · Medic · Gate · Zones`. One tap, instant filter, no dropdown menus. |
| **Frictionless taste personalization** | Instead of a long quiz, DICE offers to connect Spotify or Apple Music for quick, accurate personalization. | You already have the three-card vibe picker, which is faster. Steal the *principle*: one tap, not a form. Add an optional "Connect Spotify" as a skip-able later step for the 92%-same-sets matching, but never make it a gate. |
| **Event detail hierarchy: critical info first, description below the fold** | DICE puts title, banner, date, time, and location at the top, highlights date/time in accent color, and pushes description and policies further down. | Your event hub should follow this exactly: event name, image, dates (in accent), venue, then "I'm going" pill. **Everything else (lineup, map, info) goes behind the segmented control, not on the home scroll.** |
| **Notify buttons on not-yet-on-sale events** | DICE puts Notify buttons on shows that have not gone on sale, folding announcements into the same feed. | Replace your "Set times haven't been published yet" apology with a tinted **"Notify me when set times drop"** pill. Same information, an action instead of a shrug. |

## Tier 3: Steal the philosophy, not the feature

**1. One accent color, reserved for the primary action.** DICE keeps backgrounds true black so poster colors pop, and reserves its signature yellow-green almost exclusively for the primary action. You said keep your colors, and you should. But steal the discipline: your accent color should appear on exactly one thing per screen, the thing you want the user to tap. Right now your app spreads visual weight evenly, which is why nothing stands out.

**2. Encode rules into the UI, not the terms of service.** DICE makes scalping structurally impossible by removing the ability to set a price entirely, there is no resale marketplace screen, no price field, no bidding. The anti-scalping stance lives in the absence of a UI control, not a policy page.

**Translation:** your privacy model should work the same way. Do not write a paragraph explaining that you delete location history. **Make it architecturally impossible to store history**, and show the absence: a "No location history stored" line in the privacy screen, not a promise. The UI should *demonstrate* the rule, not describe it.

**3. Delayed reveal as a trust signal.** DICE withholds the ticket barcode until shortly before doors, showing a countdown and an animated placeholder, then activates it with motion near showtime. Security expressed through timing.

**Translation:** your post-event recap should unlock at +1 day, not immediately. The locked state with a countdown ("Recap unlocks tomorrow") builds anticipation and makes the reveal feel earned. The same principle applies to the crew card: generate it only after the event ends, so it is a memory, not a preview.

## What NOT to steal

| Do not steal | Why |
|---|---|
| **Phone number signup** | DICE's reliance on phone number for sign-up is a friction point even in their own reviews, and Browse First only partly mitigates it. You are guest-first. Keep it. |
| **Ticketing, payments, waitlist, resale** | Not your product. You link out to Howler. Do not rebuild checkout. |
| **Merch drops and VIP meet-and-greets** | DICE has them because it is a commerce platform. You are a coordination tool. Cut. |
| **"Recently Viewed" eating the screen** | DICE's own design critique calls this an unbalanced visual hierarchy that pulls attention away from search. Do not copy their mistake. |
| **Map hidden behind a corner button** | Also flagged in DICE's critique as making the map feel secondary. Your map must be a tab, not a button. |

---

## The one-sentence steal list

**Steal the poster-as-interface home feed, the browse-before-name sequence, the fixed bottom primary action, the pill filter chips, the critical-info-first event hierarchy, and the philosophy of encoding rules into the UI instead of the copy, and leave the phone-number signup, the ticketing flow, the waitlist, and the recently-viewed section behind, because DICE's presentation discipline is world-class and its commerce surface is somebody else's problem.**
